Udemy vs Skillshare: Which Learning Platform is Right For You?

By Sarah Williams | March 18,2025

With so many online learning platforms available, choosing between Udemy and Skillshare can be challenging. Here's a detailed comparison to help you decide which one suits your learning needs.

Course Selection

Udemy: Udemy stands out in the online education market with its incredibly vast catalog of over 210,000 courses across a wide range of disciplines, including technology, business management, marketing, finance, music production, photography, health, personal development, and much more. Each course is individually curated and created by industry professionals, passionate educators, entrepreneurs, and even hobbyists who bring their unique experiences and perspectives to the content they deliver. This decentralized approach to content creation leads to an incredibly diverse collection of teaching styles, content depths, and course structures. Learners can find highly specialized courses that target niche skills, as well as broader foundational courses for beginners. The platform also features learning paths that bundle related courses together to guide learners through a structured skill development journey. In addition to technical skills, Udemy has also expanded to offer courses on soft skills like leadership, emotional intelligence, communication, and negotiation. Unlike traditional education models, Udemy provides lifetime access to purchased courses, allowing learners to progress at their own pace without the stress of deadlines. This feature is particularly beneficial for working professionals, busy students, or hobbyists who prefer a flexible schedule.

Skillshare: Skillshare focuses more narrowly on creative, artistic, and entrepreneurial skills, offering a catalog of over 35,000 classes. These classes cover a rich assortment of creative fields such as graphic design, photography, illustration, animation, writing, branding, productivity, and entrepreneurship. The platform positions itself as a hub for fostering creativity, encouraging users to not only consume content but also actively engage with it through practical projects. Unlike Udemy, Skillshare operates on a subscription-based model, granting learners unlimited access to the entire course library. Instructors on Skillshare often design their classes to be concise and highly project-oriented, making them perfect for learners who prefer hands-on experiences over theoretical lectures. The community-driven aspect of Skillshare sets it apart, as students can share their work, receive feedback, and connect with peers around the world. Whether you’re an aspiring artist, freelancer, side hustler, or creative professional looking to expand your skill set, Skillshare provides a nurturing environment tailored for practical, collaborative learning.

Pricing Structure

Udemy: Udemy’s pricing strategy revolves around a pay-per-course model where individual courses are priced anywhere from $10 to $200 depending on the subject, course length, instructor reputation, and production quality. However, Udemy frequently runs aggressive promotional campaigns, offering deep discounts where premium courses can be purchased for as low as $9.99 or $11.99. During seasonal sales like Black Friday, New Year, and Back-to-School events, students can avail themselves of massive savings. The lifetime access model ensures that once you purchase a course, you can revisit it as many times as you like, which is especially helpful for learners who prefer revisiting content or learning at their own pace. Some courses even come with bonus materials like downloadable resources, coding exercises, and community Q&A sessions. In addition to free and paid courses, Udemy for Business offers an enterprise-level subscription package tailored for corporate teams, granting access to curated learning paths designed to enhance employee skills and organizational productivity.

Skillshare: Skillshare, in contrast, adopts a subscription-based model with a flat fee of around $15 per month or an annual plan priced at roughly $99 per year, offering substantial savings for long-term subscribers. This subscription grants unlimited access to the platform’s entire library of courses, allowing users to explore diverse fields without additional costs. New users can take advantage of free trial periods ranging from 7 days to one month, depending on promotions, enabling them to test the platform before committing financially. Moreover, Skillshare occasionally collaborates with companies, universities, and influencers to provide scholarships, discounts, and bundled membership offers. The subscription model particularly benefits highly curious learners who like to jump between subjects and explore a wide range of interests without worrying about individual course fees. However, if you stop paying the subscription, access to courses is revoked, which can be a drawback compared to Udemy’s lifetime access model.

Teaching Style

Udemy: Udemy courses are generally designed to offer a structured, comprehensive learning experience. Instructors usually create in-depth syllabi that cover topics from basic concepts to advanced techniques in a logical, progressive order. Courses often include several hours of pre-recorded video content supplemented with assignments, downloadable materials like e-books and practice files, coding exercises (for tech-related subjects), quizzes to test understanding, and sometimes live Q&A sessions. Upon successful completion of a course, students receive a certificate of completion, which can be showcased on resumes, portfolios, or LinkedIn profiles. The wide variety of instructors means that while some courses are highly polished and professional, others might be more casual and conversational, catering to different learning preferences. For career-focused learners, many courses offer preparation for certifications like AWS Certified Developer, PMP, or Microsoft Azure Fundamentals, blending theoretical knowledge with practical application to meet professional standards.

Skillshare: Skillshare classes are designed to be short, practical, and heavily project-based. Most classes are broken into bite-sized lessons that focus on applying learned concepts immediately through hands-on projects. Students are encouraged to upload their work to the project gallery, where they can receive constructive feedback from peers and instructors. This project-centric approach fosters active learning, creativity, and community engagement. Classes typically last between 30 minutes and 2 hours, making them ideal for learners who prefer quick, actionable learning sessions rather than lengthy theoretical lectures. Instructors often prioritize real-world application over theory, ensuring that students leave the class having created something tangible — whether it’s a digital illustration, a website wireframe, a short film, or a personal productivity plan. Moreover, the community aspect of Skillshare, combined with discussion forums, monthly challenges, and collaborative workshops, makes it a vibrant space for creatives to connect, collaborate, and grow together.

Best For

Udemy: Udemy is an excellent choice for individuals who seek structured learning experiences aimed at building professional skills, preparing for certifications, or mastering technical subjects. Whether you’re a software developer looking to learn a new programming language, a business professional aiming to enhance your management skills, or a student preparing for competitive exams, Udemy offers courses that align with serious academic or professional growth goals. Its breadth of topics also makes it a good resource for self-starters who enjoy learning independently at their own pace. Additionally, since Udemy courses offer lifetime access, they’re perfect for people who appreciate the ability to revisit course material years later without needing to renew a subscription.

Skillshare: Skillshare is best suited for individuals driven by creative passion and a desire for hands-on learning. It’s ideal for hobbyists, freelancers, entrepreneurs, visual learners, and anyone interested in acquiring creative and practical skills in a relaxed, engaging environment. Whether you want to improve your drawing skills, learn how to edit videos, start a YouTube channel, write a novel, or master social media marketing, Skillshare offers diverse classes tailored to these needs. The emphasis on project-based learning ensures that students not only consume knowledge but actively apply it. Moreover, the community aspect adds an extra layer of motivation and support, making Skillshare an excellent choice for those who thrive in interactive, collaborative learning spaces.

If you need structured technical training, Udemy may be better. For creative skills and community learning, Skillshare excels. Consider your learning goals and budget when choosing.
